# Phase 1 synthesis — hypothesis discovery

Lead agent, 2026-11-14. Inputs: seven independent specialist files in `research/agent_findings/P1_*.md`. Output register: `research/hypotheses.md` (45 hypotheses).

## Process

- Seven specialists investigated independently with no leading hypothesis (DEC-002).
- A usage limit interrupted all seven; they were resumed from saved state. Search budgets were cut short for most (5-20 searches against 15-30 planned); the causal specialist completed 35.
- **Evidence quality:** all verification is from search-result summaries, not full texts. Treat every number as provisional until checked against the primary source.

## Consensus (independently reached by two or more specialists)

1. **No single binding constraint.** Planning, construction and public-finance specialists each found that the constraint that binds differs by city and by period. Entitlement/approval, financial viability and construction capacity each have supporting evidence in some regime.
2. **Accounting shares are not causal effects.** Development-charge and "government charges" shares of price (8-24%) come largely from industry-commissioned work and say nothing about incidence.
3. **Recent Canadian reforms are unevaluated.** No design-based Canadian estimate was found for: upzoning (BC Bills 44/47, Calgary, Edmonton, Toronto), development-charge changes or incidence, the Housing Accelerator Fund, the GST rental rebate / MLI Select, the 2018 Ontario rent-control exemption, or the 2024 student cap.
4. **Canadian causal literature is strongest on demand-side taxes and credit rules, weakest on supply regulation** (causal and economist specialists).
5. **Credible identification sits at sub-municipal thresholds** (distance rings, lot-size and vintage cutoffs, municipal borders), not at province or CMA level, where treatments are bundled and clusters are few.
6. **Completions cannot yet identify effects of 2023-25 reforms.** Leading outcomes (land prices, applications, permits) can.
7. **Time-varying regulation is not measured nationally.** MLURS is a single 2022 wave; there is no national zoning atlas.

## Unresolved uncertainty

- Whether parcel-level land-price and assessment data (BC Assessment, MPAC, Teranet) are obtainable at acceptable cost.
- Whether unit-level rent and first-occupancy data exist for the Ontario 2018 cutoff.
- Whether building permits by dwelling type are published monthly at municipality (CSD) level.
- Coverage of development-charge schedules before 2019 and outside Ontario.
- Singapore, Netherlands, Germany, Australia, NZ MDRS outcomes: not researched (international search budget truncated).

## Cross-examination: what the specialists' findings imply together

1. The hypotheses that are both **policy-central and unevaluated** cluster in four areas: development charges (H26-H28), upzoning/entitlement (H15, H16, H18), demand shock and rental market (H01, H02, H42), and pipeline stage/viability (H17, H34, H35, H41).
2. The **regime-dependence** finding suggests that single-mechanism questions ("is zoning the problem?") are mis-posed. A stage-of-pipeline framing matches the charter's system view.
3. The strongest identification (BC TOA rings, Ontario rent-control cutoff) depends on data whose access is unproven. Feasibility with open data is the main discriminator for Phase 3 and is the focus of Phase 2.
4. Several widely cited magnitudes trace to industry or advocacy sources (BILD/Altus, Fraser, BuildForce, WIFO). These are leads, not evidence.

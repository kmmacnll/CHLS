# P2 — Pipeline stage binding: capacity vs viability vs entitlement (Construction/Development Economist)

Date: 2026-11-14. 22 web searches. Search tool only returned snippets; WebFetch to statcan.gc.ca failed (host not in allowed network). Nothing below was read from a primary PDF unless stated; secondary-source items are tagged. `[V-sec]` = seen in search summary of primary or reputable secondary; `[unverified]` = not confirmed this session.

## 0. Bottom line
- The cluster supports a rigorous **descriptive** study and a **partial** causal study (tenure shift around GST/MLI; rate-shock exposure). It does **not** support clean city-by-month identification of "the binding stage" with national data, because applications and approvals are unobservable nationally and CMHC's approved-not-started series is only ~7 months old.
- The three stories are not mutually exclusive at CMA level; the testable object is **regime-dependent stage conversion**, not a single winner.

### Disagreements / issues to preserve
- Unbuilt inventory sizes differ by 8x across sources (CMHC 144k permitted-not-started national; Toronto 297k approved city-only; CivicAction 1.2M GTHA) because they count different stages. No source defines a common "approved" stage. This **is** the main measurement finding: the pipeline stage definitions are not harmonised.
- Rental vs condo: Calgary rental starts -30% H1 2026 while still ~60% of starts; Toronto/Vancouver rental up. Falling asking rents in four large CMAs reported (secondary). Rental-viability is therefore also deteriorating.
- Labour: record 650k workers (2023) and 2025 record starts in Calgary/Edmonton vs CMHC shortage forecasts. Not reconciled in the literature found.

## 2. Rental policy evidence (task 2)
- **GST enhanced rebate**: 36%→100% of GST on purpose-built rental; construction begun after 13 Sep 2023 (before 2031), substantially complete before 2036 (CRA, `[V-sec]`; note the CRA text I saw says 2031/2036, Phase 1 says 2030; reconcile against CRA RC4231). PBO RP-2425-003: $5.8B foregone revenue 2023-24 to 2028-29 vs Finance $4.5B; assumes rental FMV at 80% of condo; costing assumes unit volumes, not an impact evaluation.
- **Provincial matching**: ON, NS, NL confirmed by CRA for enhanced rebate (same construction-start window); NB window different (starting 15 Nov 2024 per one source); PEI conflicting (BDO yes, CRA enhanced list no, Justice regulation text includes); BC PST: no confirmed measure; QC unconfirmed. **Treatment dates are heterogeneous (Sep 2023 federal vs Nov 2024 NB) — useful for staggered design but all of it must be confirmed from CRA.** NS HST cut 15%→14% 1 Apr 2025 (separate confound).
- **ACLP**: >$29B committed, >74,500 rental units as of 31 Dec 2025 (CMHC Annual Report 2025); envelope reported raised to $55B Oct 2025 `[V-sec]`. CMHC says ACLP + multi-unit insurance supported most rental construction in 2025. **MLI Select volume: no 2025 unit figure found.** Phase 1 "~179,000 homes 2024" remains single-source and includes existing buildings.
- **Evaluation**: no causal evaluation of GST rebate or MLI Select located (CMHC, PBO, Desjardins, academic). Only descriptive: rental starts overtook condo in Toronto H1 2026 (first since 1994); Vancouver rental ~60% of starts 2026 vs <20% 2016.
- **Net-new vs tenure shift**: not answerable from published work. Total starts nationally: CMHC 259k in 2025 forecast to ~247k 2026; so rental surge has not offset condo fall at the national total in 2026. This is consistent with partial substitution plus no net addition 2026, but also consistent with rates/demand; no counterfactual.

## 4. Inference feasibility (task 4)

### Accounting identities and definitional mismatches
- UC_t = UC_{t-1} + starts_t − completions_t − (cancellations/ stalls/ reclassifications). With completions measured only in CMAs since 2023 and SCS revising, identity holds only approximately; **residual is not zero** and must be modelled, not assumed. Dataset-consistency check is a required first step (test whether Δ UC − starts + completions ≈ 0 by CMA).
- Permits ≠ starts: permits recorded at issuance by municipalities (units, value), starts recorded at CMHC-observed construction beginning (SCS field enumeration; excavation/foundation criterion `[unverified]` for CMHC, US uses excavation). Permits lapse (typically 6–12 months without commencement, province-dependent `[unverified]`), phased permits, conversions (excluded from CMHC pending-starts), and non-permit jurisdictions create mismatch. Municipalities in StatCan permits survey vs CMHC's SCS coverage (centres ≥10k) differ.
- Conversion rate estimates therefore need a **hazard framing** (monthly start hazard of permitted-unstarted stock, by structure type, with censoring), not ratio of flows. Lag distribution identifiable only with vintage of permit cohorts; CMHC's new stock series cannot supply this; must reconstruct stock by cumulating StatCan permits minus starts (error accumulates; needs benchmark from CMHC stock series for calibration over 2026 months).
- Structure-type mix: high-rise condo permit→start lag very different (financing/presale gating) from rental/freehold; must stratify.

### Exogenous variation available
| Source of variation | Separates | Design | Weakness |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2022–23 rate shock × pre-shock exposure to presale-financed condos (share of 2021 UC or permits that were condo in CMA) | viability vs capacity | Shift-share / DiD on starts and conversion by CMA; exposure measured pre-2022 | Exposure correlated with price level, investor share, immigration; rate shock common across CMAs so identification relies on exposure heterogeneity only; GST/MLI hits same units post-Sep 2023 |
| GST rebate by tenure × province matching × date | tenure shift vs net add | Triple difference (rental/condo × matching/non-matching × pre/post) | Relabelling of condo as rental; MLI/ACLP concurrent; few provincial clusters (ON, NS, NL, NB, maybe PEI); intended-market is CMHC-observed at start, may be endogenous; start-date bunching (13 Sep 2023) testable |
| MLI Select point-threshold bunching | rental viability | needs CMHC microdata | Not public |
| Construction wage/vacancy shocks | capacity | Province×quarter vacancy rates vs conversion | Vacancy is endogenous to demand; need supply-side instruments (immigration of tradespeople, Alberta oil shock) `[hypothesis]`; ER-level data may be missing |
| Municipal process reforms (Ontario Bill 109 timelines; DC cuts 2026 per Desjardins) | entitlement | event study on application→approval where application data exist | Only in cities with application data; Ontario DC cuts (2026) too recent for post-window |
| Construction cost shocks (BCPI) | cost | Cost pass-through to viability | BCPI aggregated across CMAs, endogenous to activity |

### Verdicts
**(a) Identify binding constraint by city and period from pipeline flows: not feasible as a nationwide causal/structural exercise; feasible with caveats as a *descriptive stage-diagnostic* at CMA×quarter for selected CMAs.** Reason: entitlement stage unobservable nationally; stock series too short; permits↔starts mismatch; flows are equilibrium outcomes and cannot reveal "which constraint binds" without a model. Defensible deliverable: conversion hazards by CMA×structure type×year with a rule-based classification (slack at stage k if stock is accumulating while downstream stock is flat), explicitly labelled as diagnostic.
**(b) Regime dependence (capacity 2021–22, viability 2023–26): feasible with caveats (descriptive/predictive); causal only for the viability arm.** Capacity binding in 2021–22 is supported only by indirect evidence here (cost inflation spike, vacancy rates); I found no direct source and cost inflation was moderate (BCPI ~2.7–2.8% y/y 2026), vacancy data not retrieved. Test: structural break in conversion hazard around the rate shock and a stage-specific interaction with local vacancy/wage tightness.
**(c) GST rebate / MLI Select net add vs tenure shift: feasible with caveats, best-identified part.** Triple-difference using CMHC intended-market starts by CMA/province; net-add test is on **total** starts in treated vs control provinces, plus condo-to-rental share. Caveats: few treated provinces, concurrent MLI/ACLP, relabelling at start, rate shock heterogeneity. Cannot identify rental effect of MLI Select separately from GST at national level without CMHC microdata.

### What would falsify each story
| Story | Falsified if |
|---|---|
| Entitlement binds | Approved/permitted stock rises while start hazard falls without rise in pro forma viability (i.e. hazard fall absent in rental/MLI-financed projects or in markets with zero rate-exposure); or approval-time reforms produce no start increase where permitted stock is already large. Weak test: approval-time effects only identifiable where application data exist |
| Viability/financing binds | Presale-reliant and rental projects show the same start-hazard decline after 2022; hazard unrelated to pre-shock condo exposure; hazard recovers after cap-rate/rate relief without cost change; or conversion of permitted stock is high (>~90% within 12 months) in markets with falling presales |
| Capacity binds | Vacancy/wage tightness in construction does not predict lower conversion or longer completion time after controlling for demand; record starts coincide with high vacancy (as in Calgary/Edmonton 2025); or UC durations lengthen without wage pressure |
All three can hold simultaneously across stages; pre-register the hierarchical classification so a null on one does not default to another.

## 5. Leakage and measurement for predictive modelling (task 5)
- **Look-ahead through revisions**: StatCan/CMHC series revised (US Census revised AUTHNOT back to 2021; SCS and permits revised). Use vintages (real-time) or lag-adjusted; otherwise models see data not available at forecast date.
- **Permits as predictor of starts**: leakage across overlapping cohorts if random cross-validation; use blocked time-series CV by CMA and by permit cohort; do not predict starts using same-month permits issued for those starts.
- **Accounting-identity leakage**: UC and completions are mechanically linked to starts; predicting completions from UC is partially tautological; benchmark against lagged-starts-only model.
- **Survey break**: completions collection limited to CMAs from 1 Jan 2023; non-CMA series discontinuity; restrict panel to CMAs.
- **Intended-market misclassification**: tenure set at start, rebates create incentive to classify as rental; features based on current-vintage labels leak policy outcomes.
- **Press/forecast variables** (CMHC outlook, Urbanation commentary) contain forward information; exclude.
- **Small CMAs**: zeros and lumpiness (single projects dominate monthly starts); aggregate to quarter and weight; apartments in particular are large-block events.
- **Regime shifts**: 2022–23 rate shock and policy changes make out-of-sample (2023–26) performance non-stationary; report errors separately by regime; benchmark with seasonal naive and ARIMA before anything complex (CLAUDE.md priority).
- **Multiple-source stage mismatch**: permit units (StatCan) vs approved units awaiting start (CMHC) vs municipal approvals are different objects; features must be named by source.

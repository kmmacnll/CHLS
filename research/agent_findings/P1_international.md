# P1 — International Comparative Evidence (Phase 1)

Scope: reforms/institutional differences with quantitative evaluations. Search-verified = confirmed via WebSearch this session (snippets/abstracts only; full papers not read). Items marked [unverified] are from memory or were not confirmed. Search budget cut short by a usage limit; several requested cases (Singapore HDB, Netherlands, Montana/Austin, Australia Accord, NZ MDRS outcome, construction-productivity cross-country, Ryan-Collins, Glaeser-Gyourko, Pennington, Li, Rodriguez-Pose & Storper) were NOT verified and appear only as flagged leads.

## Case evaluations

### 1. Auckland Unitary Plan upzoning (2016), NZ
- Policy: citywide upzoning, ~3/4 of residential land opened to medium/high density.
- Design/estimate: Greenaway-McGrevy & Phillips (parcel-level, upzoned vs. non-upzoned locations) attribute ~22,000 consented homes 2016-2021 to upzoning; Greenaway-McGrevy & So (synthetic-control-type, Auckland vs. other NZ urban areas) report 3-bedroom rents 26-33% lower six years on than counterfactual, smaller for 2-bed (as reported by Auckland Council chief economist; interest.co.nz summary). Consents per 1000 residents 5.9 (1996-2016) vs 9.5 (2017-2023) (Council economist; descriptive).
- URLs: https://ourauckland.aucklandcouncil.govt.nz/news/2024/03/auckland-upzoning-sparks-more-homes-and-improved-affordability/ ; https://motu-www.motu.org.nz/wpapers/24_07.pdf ; https://www.rnz.co.nz/news/top/513134/auckland-unitary-plan-improving-housing-affordability-research-shows
- Critiques: results depend on comparison-city construction; rent paper partly working-paper status; coincident shocks (rates, migration swings) and national policy changes; absolute affordability still poor. Peer review status of rent result [unverified].
- Canada hypothesis: broad, by-right, citywide upzoning (not parcel-by-parcel rezoning) raises permits where land is scarce and demand high.
- Transferability: unitary council + national override vs. Canadian provincial/municipal split; NZ has no local development-charge dependency of Canadian type.
- Canadian analogue: Toronto/Ontario "Growing Greener"-era multiplexes, Vancouver Broadway/Making Room, Edmonton zoning bylaw renewal (2024), BC Bill 44 (SSMUH), Ontario Bill 23. Testable with permits panels. [analogues named from memory; unverified in this session]

### 2. Minneapolis 2040 Plan
- Policy: ended single-family-only zoning, removed parking minimums, up-zoned transit corridors (2019-2021).
- Design: Gu & Munro (Middlebury; synthetic control; working paper per press) — rents 17.5-34% and prices 16-34% below counterfactual over ~5 years, BUT construction was weaker than synthetic Minneapolis, so authors point to demand weakness/expectations, not supply. Minneapolis Fed synthetic-control tracker cautions about confounding. Iowa State 2021 study found +3-5% prices (competing). Single-family-zoning change accounts for ~1% of new permitted units (Bloomberg column via search; secondary).
- URLs: https://minneapolisfed.org/article/2025/unpacking-supply-and-demand-in-rent-trends-since-the-minneapolis-2040-plan ; https://www.startribune.com/mpls-2040-plan-affordable-housing-policy-reform/601455039 ; https://marginalrevolution.com/marginalrevolution/2025/08/did-the-minnesota-housing-reform-lower-housing-costs.html
- Contested: headline affordability gains may not come from the reform's supply channel; co-timed events (2020 demand shock, rent stabilization debates in St. Paul as neighbouring comparison) confound. Evidence quality: CONTESTED.
- Canada hypothesis: eliminating single-detached zoning alone yields little incremental supply; bundled multifamily/transit upzoning and parking reform drive any effect.
- Transferability: US synthetic controls use many donor metros; Canada has few comparable CMAs (small donor pool).

### 3. Houston lot-size reform (1998/1999) and townhome conversions
- Policy: by-right minimum lot size inside Loop 610 cut 5,000 -> 3,500 sq ft; opt-out ("minimum lot size area") option; small-lot subdivisions.
- Design: Hamilton (Mercatus 2023) difference-in-differences: no evidence assessed land values rose; UT Austin/Pew study: conversions raised household counts in high-demand areas without evident displacement.
- URLs: https://www.mercatus.org/publications/urban-economics/liberalizing-land-use-regulations-case-houston ; https://www.theregreview.org/2020/11/25/masuda-farkas-land-use-reform-more-housing-houston/
- Critiques: sponsor orientation (Mercatus/Pew advocacy-adjacent); opt-out regime allowed protected enclaves; Houston's baseline (no zoning, abundant land, cheap greenfield) is atypical. Quantified price effects thinner than for Auckland.
- Canada hypothesis: small increments in by-right lot flexibility may produce infill without raising land prices if applied geographically broadly (relevant to Ontario/BC as-of-right 3-4 units).
- Transferability: Texas annexation, no provincial-style bureaucracy; MUD financing of infrastructure.
- Canadian analogue: BC SSMUH legislation (2024) with staged municipal adoption — a natural staggered adoption design.

### 4. Zurich upzoning (Büchler & Lutz)
- Design: parcel-level upzoning in Zurich canton; upzoning by >=20% -> +13% housing supply on treated parcel over 10 years; no significant local rent change vs. untreated parcels in the same market.
- URL: https://ideas.repec.org/p/arz/wpaper/2022_250.html ; https://www.lewis.ucla.edu/2024/12/11/83-local-effects-of-upzoning-with-elena-lutz-and-simon-buchler
- Critiques: working paper; parcel-level null on rents is expected under citywide equilibrium; modest 13% effect implies weak response (slow, redevelopment constrained by existing tenants/ownership fragmentation). Evidence supports "upzoning works but slowly," not rapid price effects.
- Canada hypothesis: upzoning response is concentrated in high-rent/high-demand locations and scales with allowed density; assembly/strata fragmentation (condo titles) may slow Canadian response.
- Transferability: strong tenant protections, cooperative ownership, direct democracy referendums.

### 5. US new-supply / filtering evidence (Asquith-Mast-Reed; Mast)
- Design: AMR (Review of Economics and Statistics 2023): large market-rate buildings in low-income tracts reduce nearby rents 5-7% relative to trend (three identification approaches incl. timing/permit-opening variation). Mast (JUE Insight 2023): migration chains from 686 buildings in 12 cities; ~70 vacancies in lower-income tracts per 100 new market units (descriptive migration chains, not causal on rents).
- URLs: https://ideas.repec.org/a/tpr/restat/v105y2023i2p359-375.html ; https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/juecon/v133y2023ics0094119021000656.html ; https://www.philadelphiafed.org/-/media/FRBP/Assets/working-papers/2020/wp20-07.pdf
- Related: Helsinki and Swedish moving-chain studies (JUE Insight 2023, https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/juecon/v133y2023ics0094119022001048.html) report chains reach lower-income households quickly.
- Critiques: excludes subsidized/LIHTC buildings; local effect not equal to metro-wide price effects; ignores induced in-migration/household formation (baseline Mast model); amenity effects heterogeneous. Pennington (SF) and Li (NYC) studies — [unverified, not searched].
- Canada hypothesis: new market-rate completions lower nearby rents; testable with CMHC rent data and building-level completions.
- Canadian analogue: pre-sale condo completions in Toronto/Vancouver; rent-control exemption for post-2018 units in Ontario complicates this.

### 6. California SB 9 and ADU reforms
- Policy: SB 9 (2021) ministerial duplex/lot-split; ADU liberalization (2016-2019) with fee/owner-occupancy relief.
- Evidence: Terner Center estimated >700,000 newly feasible homes (2021 modelling); early permit data ("first year") showed very limited SB 9 uptake across 13 cities; ADU uptake far larger where local rules were permissive (fees, owner-occupancy, size).
- URLs: https://ternercenter.berkeley.edu/research-and-policy/sb-9-turns-one-applications/ ; https://planetizen.com/news/2023/01/121105-analysis-californias-single-family-zoning-killer-barely-making-dent-so-far
- Lesson: legal permission without financial feasibility, fee relief and local implementation yields little ("failed/underperforming" case). Builder's remedy [unverified, not searched].
- Canada hypothesis: Ontario/BC by-right small-scale density will underperform where lot economics, DCs and financing do not pencil; implementation details dominate legal text.
- Canadian analogue: BC SSMUH, Ontario 3-unit as-of-right (2023), Canada's Housing Accelerator Fund (HAF) — compare uptake across municipalities.

### 7. Berlin Mietendeckel (rent freeze/cap, 2020-2021)
- Design: Hahn, Kholodilin, Waltl, Fongoni (Management Science 2024; DIW DP 1928/1999): difference-in-differences on listings vs. unregulated comparison (Potsdam etc.). Advertised rents fell; number of available rental listings fell significantly (part permanently lost to rental sector); rent gaps and rising rents in neighbouring municipalities; authors conclude costs to renters outweigh benefits. Struck down by Constitutional Court (federal competence) after one year.
- URLs: https://www.diw.de/de/diw_01.c.873005.de/publikationen/externe_referierte_aufsaetze/2024_0003/forward_to_the_past__short-term_effects_of_the_rent_freeze_in_berlin.html ; https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:diw:diwwpp:dp1928
- Critiques: short window; listings are a measure of advertised not realized rentals; IW Köln survey (industry-adjacent) reports ~52% fewer ads, ~10% rent reduction.
- Canada hypothesis: strict rent caps reduce mobility/supply of rental listings; relevant to provincial rent-control design (vacancy decontrol vs. unit-based).
- Canadian analogue: Ontario 2018 removal of rent control on post-Nov-2018 units; BC/Quebec regimes; Ontario Bill 184 etc. Compare provinces. Quebec's TAL guideline regime is a stable, long-run cross-province variation.

### 8. Tokyo / Japan national zoning system
- Policy: national zoning code (12 categories, nationally set permissive maximums), 2002 Urban Renaissance Act; housing stock in Tokyo nearly tripled 1963-2013 (advocacy source).
- Evidence: mostly descriptive/advocacy (Reason, Mises, Next City). Counter: aging/shrinking population and depreciating-structure/demolition culture drive low prices; living space per income remains modest. No credible causal quasi-experiment located in this session.
- URLs: https://nextcity.org/urbanist-news/japan-shows-the-way-to-affordable-megacities ; https://www.centreforcities.org/blog/can-tokyo-show-us-how-to-solve-britains-housing-
- Evidence quality: WEAK/DESCRIPTIVE. Do not treat as evidence that national zoning caused affordability.
- Canada hypothesis: centralizing zoning authority (provincial templates) increases supply elasticity; testable only weakly (Ontario/BC provincial overrides).
- Transferability: very low (demographics, land law, building depreciation, rail-led development).

### 9. Vienna limited-profit housing (Gemeinnuetziger Wohnbau) and Austria
- Evidence: WIFO study finds price-dampening effect of limited-profit housing on market rents; new LPH units ~27% cheaper than private rentals (advocacy-linked report). Design appears to be correlational/hedonic; commissioned by sector. 
- URLs: https://www.wifo.ac.at/en/news/the-price-dampening-effect-of-non-profit-housing/ ; https://www.wifo.ac.at/wp-content/uploads/upload-2157/rb_2023_006_.pdf
- Critiques: selection into LPH tenancy; long accumulated stock (a century); cross-subsidy via revolving funds and cost-rent; not a reform that can be dated.
- Canada hypothesis: scaled non-profit/cost-rent supply with long-run revolving capital moderates market rents; Canadian non-profit share is tiny, so test via Canadian co-op/non-profit stock variation across cities.
- Canadian analogue: BC Housing/Housing Hub, CMHC Affordable Housing Fund, Montreal 20-20-20 bylaw. Evaluation [unverified].

### 10. Sweden (rent regulation + queue) and Helsinki/Finland land policy
- Sweden: rent-regulated Stockholm allocated by queue; average wait ~9 years; access determined by queue time, not income (KTH working paper; 2024 Housing Studies structural demand paper finds high willingness to pay for regulated flats). Evidence descriptive/structural; no clean policy shock located.
- URLs: https://swopec.hhs.se/kthrec/abs/kthrec2021_004.htm ; https://ideas.repec.org/a/taf/chosxx/v39y2024i12p3092-3116.html
- Helsinki: city owns ~64-70% of land within boundary (sources differ), leases land, Hitas price cap; prices still rose markedly in 2010s; ARA lot prices published. No quasi-experimental evaluation found; Hitas critiques exist. 
- URLs: https://www.housing2030.org/?p=2173 ; https://www.hel.fi/static/kanslia/Julkaisut/Kotikaupunkina-Helsinki/2020/Implementation_Programme_on_Housing_and_Related_Land_Use.pdf
- Canada hypothesis: municipal land ownership/value capture lets cities finance infrastructure and sustain production; Canadian cities own less and rely on DCCs and property tax.
- Canadian analogue: Vancouver/CMHC public land programs, federal Build Canada Homes (2025) [unverified]. 

### 11. NZ: MDRS/NPS-UD and reversal
- Policy: 2021 Medium Density Residential Standards mandated 3 storeys/3 units in major cities; later government moved to make MDRS optional for councils (Epoch Times report; date/detail not confirmed). 
- Search for formal evaluations returned nothing usable. Treat as [unverified]: status of reversal, uptake, and permit effects.
- Canada hypothesis: top-down mandates are politically fragile when local resistance and elections intervene; durable reform may require financial incentives (HAF-style) rather than mandates.
- Canadian analogue: Ontario Bill 23 partial rollbacks (2023-2024) and provincial reversal of greenbelt moves [unverified].

### 12. Financialization / credit explanation (cross-country)
- Evidence: Jorda, Schularick & Taylor, "The Great Mortgaging" (Economic Policy 2016; NBER WP 20501): 17 economies 1870-2011; mortgage lending roughly doubled share of bank balance sheets over 20th century; real-estate lending booms precede deeper recessions. House-price-level causal claims: only indirect in what I verified. Ryan-Collins [unverified, not searched].
- URLs: https://www.frbsf.org/research-and-insights/publications/working-papers/2014/09/the-great-mortgaging-housing-finance-crises-and-business-cycles/ ; https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:nbr:nberwo:20501
- Competing: supply-elasticity (Glaeser-Gyourko) and demand-side credit/interest rate stories are not mutually exclusive; credit expansion raises prices more where supply is inelastic (Mian-Sufi-type results [unverified]).
- Canada hypothesis: credit/interest-rate and supply elasticity interact; test heterogeneity of mortgage-rate shocks by local supply elasticity across CMAs.

### 13. Aggregate-growth critique of supply liberalization (Hsieh-Moretti and critics)
- Hsieh & Moretti (AEJ Macro 2019, "Housing Constraints and Spatial Misallocation"): calibrated model attributing large US aggregate growth losses to housing constraints in high-productivity cities. Critics: Duranton & Puga (Econometrica 2023) reach a similar policy direction through different mechanics; calibrated/model dependent; reported replication concerns from commentators (secondary sources, blog-level). Rodriguez-Pose & Storper sceptic position: [unverified].
- URLs: https://assets.aeaweb.org/asset-server/files/7670.pdf ; https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w21154/w21154.pdf
- Canada: no direct evaluation; welfare gains from loosening Toronto/Vancouver constraints would depend on agglomeration elasticities not estimated here.

## Hypotheses (INTL)

### INTL-1 — Citywide by-right upzoning raises permits and slows rent growth
- Mechanism: relaxing binding density limits increases feasible redevelopment/infill where land values exceed construction cost plus existing-use value.
- Predicted effect: permits per capita rise (Auckland: consents/1000 ~5.9 -> 9.5 descriptive); rents 26-33% below counterfactual for 3-bed after 6 years (Auckland, SC-type design); smaller/null at parcel level (Zurich).
- Unit of analysis: municipality/CMA-year; parcel-level for treated vs. untreated.
- Required variables: zoning change dates, permits/starts, completions, rents, land values.
- Potential datasets: CMHC starts/completions and rental market survey; Statistics Canada building permits; municipal zoning bylaw archives; MPAC/BC Assessment.
- Existing literature: Greenaway-McGrevy & Phillips (Motu WP 24-07); Büchler & Lutz (ideas.repec.org/p/arz/wpaper/2022_250.html).
- Evidence against: Minneapolis effect not via supply; Zurich only +13% on treated parcels over 10 years; counterfactual dependence.
- Likely identification strategy: staggered DiD / synthetic control using Canadian municipal adoption dates (Edmonton, BC SSMUH, Ontario as-of-right, Vancouver).
- Expected difficulty: med (few CMAs; endogenous timing).
- Novelty: med (Canadian staggered reforms little evaluated).
- Policy importance: high.
- Proposed class: B partially understood

### INTL-2 — Legal permission without financial feasibility yields little (SB 9 type failure)
- Mechanism: fees (DCs/DCCs), parking, financing, process discretion and lot economics, not zoning text, bind.
- Predicted effect: uptake of as-of-right small-scale density low in high-fee/high-process municipalities; high where ADU-like fee relief exists.
- Unit: municipality-year.
- Required variables: permit counts for newly legalized forms, DC rates, approval times, lot prices.
- Datasets: municipal permit portals; provincial DC bylaws; CMHC.
- Literature: Terner Center SB 9 brief/one-year update (URLs above).
- Evidence against: SB 9 early (first-year) data; ADU success shows uptake possible; HOA constraints US-specific.
- Identification: cross-municipal variation in implementation under common provincial law (BC Bill 44, Ontario 2023).
- Difficulty: med. Novelty: med. Importance: high.
- Proposed class: B partially understood

### INTL-3 — Rent-cap design determines rental listing supply (Berlin/Ontario contrast)
- Mechanism: price ceilings reduce rental supply/listings and shift demand to unregulated segments.
- Predicted effect: negative on listings (DiD, Berlin), advertised rents fall; spillover to adjacent unregulated areas.
- Unit: listing/submarket-month; province-year.
- Required: asking rents, listings counts, tenure conversions, exemptions by build year.
- Datasets: Rentals.ca, Zumper, CMHC Rental Market Survey, provincial rent boards.
- Literature: Hahn et al. (DIW DP 1928; Management Science 2024).
- Evidence against: short window (one year); listings vs. realized rents; Canadian regimes (guideline caps with vacancy decontrol) differ structurally from a freeze.
- Identification: Ontario Nov-2018 exemption cutoff (RD on build date), BC/Quebec comparisons.
- Difficulty: med. Novelty: low-med. Importance: high.
- Proposed class: B partially understood

### INTL-4 — New market-rate supply lowers nearby and downstream rents
- Mechanism: local supply shock plus filtering/migration chains.
- Predicted: -5% to -7% nearby rents (AMR); ~0.7 vacancies per new unit in lower-income tracts (Mast).
- Unit: building/tract-year.
- Required: building completion dates, rents by tract, migration/address histories.
- Datasets: CMHC, census tract data, Teranet, building-level permit records; address-history data only via Statistics Canada RDC (availability uncertain).
- Literature: Asquith-Mast-Reed (REStat 2023); Mast (JUE 2023); Helsinki/Sweden chains.
- Against: excludes induced demand/household formation; heterogeneous amenity effects; Canadian rent control exemptions.
- Identification: event study around completion with permit-timing/nearby-unbuilt-site controls.
- Difficulty: high (data). Novelty: med-high in Canada. Importance: high.
- Proposed class: B partially understood

### INTL-5 — Single-family-zoning abolition alone is a weak supply lever
- Mechanism: multifamily rezoning, parking and corridor upzoning matter more than ending SFH exclusivity.
- Predicted: small increment (Minneapolis SF change ~1% of permitted units, secondary source).
- Unit: city/zone-year.
- Required: unit counts by zone type, bundling of reforms.
- Datasets: municipal permits; zoning layers.
- Literature: Minneapolis Fed; Gu & Munro.
- Against: Gu & Munro rent decline large; interactions.
- Identification: decomposition of permitted units by pre-reform zone.
- Difficulty: low-med. Novelty: low. Importance: med.
- Proposed class: C contested

### INTL-6 — Public land ownership / non-profit cost-rent stock moderates market rents
- Mechanism: cost-based supply reduces rents directly and by competition; land leasing sustains production through cycles.
- Predicted: 27% lower rents on new LPH units vs. private (advocacy-linked; correlation); price-dampening effect on market (WIFO).
- Unit: city-year / stock share.
- Required: non-profit stock share, land ownership share, rent indices.
- Datasets: CMHC, CHRA, municipal land inventories.
- Literature: WIFO RB 2023/006; Helsinki programmes.
- Against: selection, century-long accumulation, small Canadian non-profit share, Finland prices still rose.
- Identification: weak; long-run cross-city comparison or Canadian program discontinuities.
- Difficulty: high. Novelty: med. Importance: med-high.
- Proposed class: C contested

### INTL-7 — Credit/rate shocks raise prices more where supply is inelastic
- Mechanism: interaction of mortgage credit expansion with local supply elasticity.
- Predicted: larger price response in constrained CMAs; JST show mortgage share of bank balance sheets doubled (long-run).
- Unit: CMA-quarter.
- Required: mortgage rates/credit, supply elasticity proxies, prices.
- Datasets: Bank of Canada, OSFI, Teranet, CMHC.
- Literature: JST (Economic Policy 2016); Ryan-Collins [unverified].
- Against: demographic/immigration demand, regulation-driven supply limits are alternative.
- Identification: shift-share in credit conditions x elasticity; Bank rate surprises.
- Difficulty: med. Novelty: med. Importance: high.
- Proposed class: D under-researched (for Canada)

### INTL-8 — Top-down (provincial/national) mandates are politically fragile (NZ MDRS, Berlin)
- Mechanism: mandates without local fiscal benefit trigger reversal; durable reform requires incentives and legal competence.
- Predicted: higher reversal and lower implementation in mandate-only regimes.
- Unit: reform episode.
- Evidence: Berlin Mietendeckel struck down; NZ MDRS being made optional (unconfirmed detail).
- Literature: thin; case-based.
- Against: Japan national zoning durable; Ontario/BC mandates persisting so far.
- Identification: comparative case study; HAF (incentive) vs. Bill 23/44 (mandate) adoption.
- Difficulty: med. Novelty: high. Importance: med.
- Proposed class: E potentially novel

## Summary
- Claims (each with confidence):
  - Upzoning raises local supply where demand is high (Auckland, Zurich): medium (design credible, magnitude varies; working papers).
  - New market-rate buildings reduce nearby rents ~5-7% (AMR): medium-high.
  - Minneapolis affordability gains attributable to supply: low (authors themselves find weak construction response).
  - Rent freeze reduces rental listings (Berlin): medium-high.
  - Legal permission alone (SB 9) yields little uptake: medium (early data only).
  - Tokyo zoning causal claim: low (descriptive/advocacy only).
  - Vienna/Helsinki institutional models lower rents: low-medium (commissioned/correlational).
- Supporting evidence: see per-case URLs above; Auckland, Zurich, AMR, Berlin have explicit quasi-experimental designs.
- Sources: Motu WP 24-07; Auckland Council; Minneapolis Fed; Star Tribune; Mercatus (Hamilton); AMR REStat 2023; Mast JUE 2023; DIW/Management Science; Terner Center; WIFO; NBER WP 20501; Hsieh-Moretti NBER 21154.
- Uncertainty: most figures from search snippets/abstracts, not full texts; peer-review status of several rent estimates unconfirmed; search budget truncated by a usage limit.
- Assumptions: counterfactual construction (synthetic controls/comparison cities) valid; Canadian institutions transfer imperfectly.
- Contradictions found: Minneapolis (Gu & Munro lower rents vs. Iowa State higher prices; no supply response); Zurich parcel-level rent null vs. Auckland metro-level rent decline (differing level of analysis); Helsinki land ownership vs. rising prices.
- Recommended next investigations: (1) verify and retrieve NZ MDRS evaluations and Australia Accord/state reform outcomes; (2) Singapore HDB, Netherlands, Germany evidence; (3) construction productivity cross-country (e.g. Allen; Goolsbee-Syverson) and Pennington/Li filtering studies; (4) design staggered DiD on BC SSMUH/Ontario as-of-right using CMHC/StatCan permits; (5) Ontario 2018 rent-control exemption RD.

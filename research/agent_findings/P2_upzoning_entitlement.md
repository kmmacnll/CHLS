## 4. Inference feasibility

Designs, ordered by credibility with open data alone:

1. **Calgary parcel DiD / triple difference (2022–2026), switch-on (Aug 2024) and switch-off (Aug 2026).** Treated: parcels in established areas that were R-C1/R-C2 etc. before Aug 2024. Controls: parcels already R-CG/H-GO/MU or exempt (already permissive, same city, same shocks). Thousands of parcels; outcome DP/BP counts, units. Pro: clean legal discontinuity in time and a second reversal. Con: single city; "already permissive" parcels are selected on being near corridors, so parallel trends is a strong assumption; SUTVA fails if developers shift assembly effort to treated parcels (displacement within city; same issue Greenaway-McGrevy modelled); rush-to-vest before 23 Mar / 4 Aug 2026 contaminates level effects; HAF (Calgary agreement), rates, migration and condo downturn concurrent; Alberta's Oct 2026 order further muddles the post-repeal period. Post-period of the repeal is only ~3 months at audit date.
2. **Edmonton parcel DiD (Jan 2024)** with Calgary or Prairie CMAs as comparison; also the pending Alberta-ordered reversion (deadline ~Jan 2027) as an anticipation test. Parking minimums (2020) confounded by COVID.
3. **BC Bill 44 staggered compliance (extensions) and lot-size 280 m² / 400 m bus cutoffs**: best for (b) because municipal fee/site-standard heterogeneity can be related to take-up. Needs a municipal panel and permit files with consistent classification; none located. Few municipalities near the 5,000 population cutoff.
4. **HAF accepters vs refusers** (Red Deer, Oakville, Vaughan, Tecumseh, Windsor vs Winnipeg, Saskatoon, Halifax etc.): treated cities in the tens, refusers a handful; selection on council politics; likely yields descriptive rather than credible causal results.
5. **Cross-city CSD annual permits (46-10-0087-01)**: staggered DiD with modern estimators; N treated cities ≈ 10–30 but treatments heterogeneous (what "four units" means varies).

Variation sufficiency: **parcel-level within-city DiD (Calgary, Edmonton) is feasible for permits; cross-city designs have few treated clusters, and cluster-robust/permutation inference is required.** Capitalization test (a, part 2) needs sales prices; open assessed values smooth and lag, so a transaction source (BC Assessment sales, MPAC, Teranet, or realtor MLS access) is likely needed [U on cost].

**Verdict: feasible with caveats** for (a) take-up by permits in Calgary and Edmonton; **feasible with caveats, weaker** for (c) durability (descriptive case-based; the NZ reversal is not confirmed, [U]); **not feasible with open data alone** for (b) as a causal claim (fees, DCs, site standards, and pro forma costs are not open at parcel level; only municipality-level cross-sectional association is attainable).

**Falsifiers**
- (a): after adjusting for pre-trends, treated-minus-control parcel permit rates after Aug 2024 are statistically indistinguishable from 0 within 24 months *and* assessed land values rise: supports "capitalizes, little construction". Opposite: treated parcels show rowhouse permits well above controls and a dose-response with lot size/frontage. Note 58% attribution (city) is a share of low-density permits, not an effect; permits displaced from other districts or from rezoning applications (460 hearings avoided) would be pure process-substitution, not net supply (see §5).
- (b): conditional on same legal permission, take-up does not vary with (fee level, parking rules, lot size, site-standard stringency) => hypothesis falsified; take-up variation across BC municipalities with identical Bill 44 text but different outcomes (Kelowna vs Coquitlam, V partial) is currently suggestive, not tested.
- (c): reforms survive politically where tied to financial incentives (HAF) but reverse where imposed; falsified if mandates (Bill 23/44) persist as well as or better than incentive-based reforms; Calgary 12–3 repeal and Alberta orders are two data points for fragility, but both are municipally enacted/provincially reversed and are not a clean mandate vs incentive contrast.

## 5. Measurement problems

- **Permits vs net new units**: Edmonton counts "net new dwellings" (demolitions netted); Calgary/Toronto permit counts may be gross. Rowhouse permits in mature neighbourhoods replace a house; net gain per permit is ~ (units − 1). Toronto's 4,880 net homes vs 2,629 permits (Phase 1, [U]) illustrates this.
- **Classification change at the reform date**: R-CG converts discretionary rezoning (land use amendment + DP) into DP-only, so applications that were previously rezonings appear as DPs; counts of "R-CG DPs" mechanically increase without new demand (478 DPs vs 460 avoided rezoning hearings is exactly this). Need to count *all* residential applications, not R-CG-labelled ones.
- **Secondary suites/garden suites**: counted as alterations or accessory in some cities, as units in others; Toronto's multiplex "permits" are 73% renovations in one analysis [V secondary]. Standardize on units created.
- **Timing**: permit issue date vs application date; reform-induced processing speed changes shift issue dates without changing starts. Permits are not starts or completions (CMHC SCS; Phase 1 leakage warning).
- **Alberta**: development permit (land-use) and building permit are separate stages; a parcel-level join needs a common roll number/address key [U].
- **Assessed vs market values** (see §3). **Rush-to-vest**: grandfathering rules in Calgary and the pending Edmonton reversion generate pre-repeal bunching of applications; model with event-time dummies, not one post indicator.
- **Source quality**: several figures above come from advocacy blogs and press; only city reports, StatCan, and journal/WP records are higher grade.

## Summary
- Claims (each with confidence):
  - Auckland provides the strongest international evidence that broad upzoning raises construction (≈ doubling pre-policy rate, 5% of stock), but the result is contested by Murray & Helm and defended by Donovan & Maltman; single-city. Medium.
  - Chicago (Freemark) and Edmonton (Zhang listing, 3–7%) show capitalization; Chicago showed no added permits in 5 years. Medium-low for transfer to Canada.
  - Edmonton permits rose sharply after the Jan 2024 bylaw (16.5k in 2024, 17.8k in 2025); the cause is not identified, city says it is not attributing causally. Medium on counts, low on attribution.
  - Calgary R-CG gave 478 DPs/1,904 units but process reclassification inflates counts. Medium.
  - Reforms are fragile: Calgary repealed 12–3 (effective 4 Aug 2026), Alberta ordered Edmonton/Calgary to revert (Oct 2026), HAF refusers lost funds. High on events, low on generalization.
  - A parcel-level DiD in Calgary/Edmonton with open permit data is feasible; (b) cannot be identified with open data alone. Medium.

**Where studies disagree.**
1. Sign on supply: Skidmore–Peddle (-25%+ development rate) and Burge–Ihlanfeldt (water/sewer fees negative) vs Burge–Ihlanfeldt (earmarked non-water fees positive in inner suburbs) and Mayer–Somerville (fees small). The sign depends on whether fees fund services that are capitalised (benefit side) — a core confound for DC cuts.
2. Incidence: US hedonics (Mathur; Evans-Cowley) find fees raise prices, including of EXISTING homes (price effect >1 in magnitude for existing in Evans-Cowley is implausible as pure incidence and signals omitted-variable/selection bias, or capitalised amenities). Ihlanfeldt–Shaughnessy: new view. Theory (Yinger-type) allows land incidence; UK evidence says land bears it. No design-based Canadian incidence estimate found.
3. All US estimates are cross-sectional/panel hedonics with endogenous fee-setting; none is a clean quasi-experiment. Treat as association with imperfect selection control.
4. Canadian "pass-through" claims (Desjardins, BILD, Dachis headline) are accounting or cross-sectional; CMHC's own 2026 modelling is viability, not realised response.

## 4. Inference feasibility

**Sizes relative to price.** Typical DC (CMHC): 8–16% of Ontario new condo price (P1, verified there); Mississauga cut ~$19k per apartment on a roughly $600–800k unit [price assumption unverified] is ~2–3%; Hamilton's up-to-$100k and Vaughan's up-to-$98k are large for detached (~8–10% of price). Metro Vancouver DCC swings of ~$7k–$9k/unit. Elasticity in the literature (Dachis 0.045 on price; Skidmore–Peddle -25% on development) is small to moderate, so a municipality-level event study needs to detect a few percent in starts against order-of-magnitude noise from 2022–26 rate/demand shocks.

**Counts.** Treated Ontario municipalities in 2024–26: roughly a dozen with distinct dated cuts (Vaughan, Mississauga, Peel, Toronto, Hamilton, Burlington, plus ~dozens after E9 by mid-2026) [counts unverified]. Because E9 pushed many to cut within weeks of each other, effective independent staggered adopters in time is small; post-period is ≤8 months.

**Controls.** In the GTHA nearly all large municipalities are treated by mid-2026; clean untreated border neighbours disappear exactly when post data begin. Untreated controls available: BC/Alberta/Quebec municipalities, small Ontario municipalities (but their markets differ), and municipalities that cut earlier vs later (staggered timing). Vancouver DCC increases/rollbacks provide an independent dose-response design but with fewer units (Metro Vancouver ~21 municipalities share regional DCCs, so the variation is common across them and only municipal DCL/CAC differ).

**Endogeneity threats.**
1. Reverse timing: cuts follow collapsing sales/permits (Ashenfelter dip); Toronto starts fell ~65% y/y in March per PFIN-10 (news), mean reversion would mimic a DC effect.
2. Province-wide simultaneity: HST relief on new homes up to $1M, GST rebate, Bill 17 deferral, HAF, rate cuts all land in the same 12 months.
3. Rate shock 2022–23 and unit-mix shifts (condo collapse vs rental/MLI) dominate local variation.
4. Upper-tier/lower-tier stacking (Peel vs Mississauga/Brampton/Caledon; York vs Vaughan/Markham): per-unit charge is the sum, and cuts at different tiers have different dates; regional DCs are not in some municipal tables.
5. Anticipation: announced scheduled increases and sunset dates cause pull-forward; rate-by-date is known months ahead. Bill 23 phase-in also created a pull-forward until 6 Jun 2024.
6. Selection of DC level by municipality (growth-capital program size) in the cross-section.
7. Pre-by-law "freeze" rules: DC rate often locked at application date (Bill 108/185 transitions), so permits issued today pay rates set at application years earlier — the treatment actually felt is the rate at application, not at permit. Mismeasures exposure.

**Verdict.**
- (a) DC effect on starts/permits: **feasible with caveats** for short-run timing/bunching around known dates (strong design: scheduled step increases, sunset deadlines, Bill 185 transition); **not feasible with open data alone** for a credible long-run level effect on starts (too few clean controls, ≤8 months post).
- (b) Incidence (price vs land): **not feasible with open data**. Needs transaction-level land/pre-development parcel prices (Teranet, MPAC, Altus) plus municipality-level new-home prices; neither is open. Could be feasible with paid data and pre-announcement tests, border-pair RD between a cut municipality and a non-cut one, and greenfield vs. serviced-lot comparisons. Metro Vancouver DCC step-ups (2025) vs. Alberta (flat) provide a useful second setting.
- (c) Retiming: **feasible** (best-identified question) using monthly CMA permits plus CSD starts and municipal portals around sunset dates (13 Nov 2026 Mississauga/Peel; Bill 185 date; scheduled indexation).

**What would falsify each.**
- (a) falsified if starts/permits in cut municipalities, relative to matched uncut, show a post-cut change within a CI that excludes the Skidmore-Peddle order (−25%) AND the CMHC viability implication (+9–14%); or if equal-magnitude responses appear in unit types/municipalities with no cut (placebo), or pre-trends diverge.
- (b) falsified (pure buyer incidence) if land values in cut areas do not rise relative to controls and new-home prices fall roughly one-for-one; (pure land incidence) if prices do not fall and land rises by the full cut. Partial pass-through is indistinguishable from noise unless price data are at municipality level.
- (c) retiming hypothesis falsified if cumulative post-cut starts exceed cumulative pre-cut deficit (net additions persist after sunset) and no post-deadline trough appears; supported if a pre-deadline spike is followed by an offsetting dip.

## 5. Leakage / measurement problems
1. **Permit vs payment vs start timing.** DCs were payable at building-permit issuance; Bill 17 (June 2025) moves non-rental payment to occupancy (in force operationally Nov 2025). Permit-issuance incentives therefore change independent of the DC rate. Permit counts after Nov 2025 are not comparable to before. [V-sec]
2. **Rate-lock at application.** Rate paid depends on application date, not permit date; transitional rules create cohorts (Bill 185). Exposure must be assigned by application/by-law date.
3. **Permits ≠ starts.** Permit issuance often leads starts by months and lapses; CMHC starts are measured at foundation stage. Permits could rise purely because developers pull permits to lock rates without starting (strategic permitting, non-economic).
4. **Units vs value.** StatCan permit value is declared at issuance; unit counts vs dollar value respond differently to DC changes (mix shift to rental/large units given discounts by type).
5. **Tier stacking and per-type rates.** Regional + municipal + education/other charges (some sources add provincial/federal); effective rate differs by dwelling type, bedrooms, and rental/non-rental status; treatment is a vector, not a scalar.
6. **FIR revenue** reflects policy-induced payment timing (Bill 17) and exemptions/discounts; using revenue as a "DC rate" proxy is circular (outcome-contaminated).
7. **Leakage in ML sense**: if starts or permits are modelled with lagged permits and DC revenue, mechanical links (permits→DC revenue) leak outcome information; DC revenue is a function of permits.
8. **Geography mismatch**: monthly StatCan permits at CMA/CA, DC regimes at municipality/upper tier, CMHC starts at CSD: aggregation can wash out treatment and mix treated/untreated CSDs within a CMA.

## 6. Recommendations (not a design commitment)
- Priority 1: retiming/bunching analysis using CMHC CSD starts and municipal permit portals around deadline dates (E2 6 Jun 2024; E5/E6 13 Nov 2026; Bill 17 Nov 2025). Low data cost.
- Priority 2: hand-build DC rate panel (by dwelling type, tier) from by-laws/background studies for ~40 municipalities 2018–2026 (the CMHC 40 list as template) — only way to get per-unit rates; log as a new dataset D#.
- Priority 3: incidence requires purchase of land/new-home price data; defer until the owner decides budget. Do not report incidence from open data.
- Pre-register the null: a no-detectable-effect on starts is a possible and informative result.

## Summary
- Claims (each with confidence):
  - No design-based Canadian estimate of DC effects on starts or incidence was found (med-high; search-limited, Baumeister/Skaburskis not retrievable).
  - US evidence is hedonic/panel with endogenous fee setting; signs conflict by fee type (water/sewer negative; amenity-type fees can be positive) (med).
  - The cleanest open-data question is retiming around known deadlines/scheduled steps (med).
  - Long-run level effects and incidence are not identifiable with open data alone because (i) post-period is ≤8 months, (ii) GTHA controls vanish after Mar 2026, (iii) no open land/new-home price data (med-high).
  - Bill 17 (non-rental DC deferral to occupancy, Royal Assent 5 Jun 2025, OBC 3 Nov 2025) changes payment timing province-wide and contaminates permit-based measures (med; secondary sources).
  - StatCan monthly building permits are probably CMA/CA-level, not CSD; CSD permits only annual (low-med; this contradicts P1's "CSD-level unverified" and needs a WDS check).
  - Metro Vancouver DCC rose sharply from Jan 2025 and was partly rolled back 24 Jul 2026: an independent natural experiment (low-med; table garbled).
- Supporting evidence: abstract-level confirmation of Ihlanfeldt–Shaughnessy, Burge–Ihlanfeldt, Mathur et al., Skidmore–Peddle, Evans-Cowley et al., Mayer–Somerville; CMHC 2025/26 Observer; Desjardins Jun 2026; policy documents via law-firm and municipal sources.
- Sources: inline URLs above.
- Uncertainty: no full texts read; per-unit amounts and effective dates for Vaughan, Mississauga, Peel, Toronto, Metro Vancouver are press-sourced and conflict; E9 (50% vs 30% threshold) unreconciled; Bill 23 rental discount % and affordable/attainable exemptions unverified; Dachis exact citation unverified; land/new-home data terms not searched.
- Assumptions: DC schedules are public by-law data that can be hand-collected; CMHC CSD starts are usable at municipality level for centres ≥10,000.
- Contradictions found: (1) P1 lists StatCan permits "CSD level unverified"; search suggests monthly is CMA-level only. (2) Evans-Cowley et al. 2009 does not include Forgey (P1/brief attribution). (3) "Dachis e-brief 281" not located; 0.45% elasticity is attributed to Dachis via C.D. Howe text. (4) Sources conflict on Vaughan's 2026 cut size and Mississauga's deadline/rental terms. (5) Federal-Ontario deal: "up to 50%" vs "at least 30%".

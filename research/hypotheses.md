### A. Demand

**H01 — Population growth outran supply [C]**
M: PR + NPR inflows raise household formation faster than completions. E: rents/prices up; modest nationally, larger in big metros. U: CMA-year, municipality. V: PR/NPR flows and stocks, rents, prices, vacancy, completions. D: StatCan population (17-10-0148/0149), IRCC, CMHC RMS, Census. L: IRCC/StatCan municipal FE-IV panel 2006-21: immigration ≈11% of price increase nationally, ≈21% in large cities; rent elasticity reported inconsistently by two specialists (0.086 vs 0.045) — to resolve in Phase 2. Literature range 0.1-1.6% price per 1% immigration. Against: estimates are PR-era; 2022-24 surge was NPR. I: shift-share (enclave) IV; NPR-policy event study. Src: ECON-1, CAUS Part 2.

**H02 — Student cap / NPR cuts lowered rents dose-responsively [E]**
M: federal cut in arrivals (22 Jan 2024) reduces rental demand most where students concentrate. E: larger 2024-26 asking-rent declines and vacancy rises in high-exposure areas; magnitude unknown. U: CMA/zone-quarter. V: pre-cap study-permit share, rents, vacancy, completions. D: IRCC permits, CMHC RMS, listing rents. L: 2025 national purpose-built vacancy 3.1% (2.2% in 2024); no causal study found. Against: record rental completions and rate cuts concurrent; Montreal rents rose despite higher vacancy. I: exposure event study with pre-trend tests, controlling for completions pipeline. Src: ECON-6, CAUS-3, C8.

**H03 — Rates and credit capitalize into prices; tightening raises rents [A-B]**
M: lower mortgage rates raise prices; tightening cuts owner demand and shifts demand to renting. E: prices down, rent inflation up after tightening; per-100bp magnitude not retrieved. U: CMA-month; national. V: mortgage rates, policy shocks, prices, CPI rent. D: BoC, Teranet, StatCan CPI. L: Rao & Wang (BoC SAP 2026-2) SVAR with external instrument; BoC SAP 2026-52: demand responds faster than supply. Price channel well established (A); rent channel newer (B). Against: cannot alone explain multi-decade price/income divergence. I: high-frequency policy surprises; B-20 (2018) DiD. Src: ECON-3, C4.

**H04 — Credit shocks × supply elasticity [D]**
M: a given credit or rate shock raises prices more where supply is inelastic. E: larger price response in constrained CMAs. U: CMA-quarter. V: rates/credit, elasticity proxies, prices, starts. D: BoC, OSFI, Teranet, CMHC. L: Rao & Wang find larger rate-driven price falls in inelastic cities; Jordà-Schularick-Taylor long-run mortgage expansion. Against: elasticity proxy confounds demand heterogeneity. I: policy surprise × pre-determined elasticity. Src: INTL-7, ECON-2/3.

**H05 — Investor and non-resident demand [B]**
M: investor and foreign purchases lift owner-occupier prices; investors also add rental stock. E: small nationally, larger locally. U: neighbourhood/CMA-month. V: investor and non-resident shares, prices. D: CHSP (46-10-0030/0054/0069), BC PTT. L: investors >20% of houses in BC, NB, NS, ON (2020); BC foreign-buyer tax −5% to −6% in exposed areas (Du et al.; Pavlov et al.) vs far larger synthetic-control estimates (Hartley et al.); Toronto LTT cut sales ~15% (Dachis, Duranton & Turner). Against: small shares; estimates differ severalfold by design. I: border and exposure DiD (largely done). Src: ECON-8, PFIN-9, C1-C5.

**H06 — Tax treatment of housing [D]**
M: untaxed principal-residence gains raise asset demand and lock-in. E: positive on prices; magnitude `[u]`. U: household/CMA. V: effective tax rates, holding periods. D: CRA, SHS. L: none retrieved. I: weak. Src: PFIN-11.

**H07 — Suppressed household formation [C]**
M: high rents force doubling-up; observed headship understates need. E: missing households concentrated in high-rent CMAs. U: CMA-census year × age. V: headship by age, rent, income. D: Census, LFS. L: PBO 631k suppressed households (2021); Toronto 250-400k (Lauster/von Bergmann). Against: TMU finds young-adult co-residence fell 2003-23; benchmark is assumption-driven. I: headship-on-rent panel with supply-shifter IV. Src: ECON-4.

**H08 — Gap estimates are benchmark-dependent [C]**
M: gap = need under a chosen benchmark minus projected stock. E: measurement hypothesis; wide plausible range. U: province/CMA projection. V: stock, household projections, benchmark. D: CMHC, PBO. L: CMHC 430-480k/yr to 2035 for 2019 affordability vs PBO 3.2M total by 2035 on a vacancy benchmark. I: replication and sensitivity. Src: ECON-5.

**H09 — Interprovincial migration arbitrage [D]**
M: high ON/BC prices push migrants to AB and Atlantic, raising prices there. E: flows track price/income gaps. U: origin-destination quarter. V: flows, price gaps, jobs. D: StatCan demography, Teranet. L: descriptive only. Against: jobs and remote work confound. I: gravity model with price-gap IV. Src: ECON-9.

### B. Supply elasticity and land

**H10 — Low supply elasticity in major CMAs [B]**
M: inelastic supply turns demand shocks into price. E: price response inverse to elasticity. U: CMA-quarter. V: starts, permits, prices, demand shocks. D: CMHC starts, permits, Teranet/NHPI. L: Paixão (BoC SAN 2021-21) proxy; secondary values Vancouver 0.63, Toronto 0.89, Winnipeg 4.34. Against: proxy assumes price-cycle differences reflect only supply. I: quantity vs price response to exogenous demand shocks. Src: ECON-2.

**H11 — Land value drives the divergence [C]**
M: price growth reflects land rents, not structure cost. E: land share rises with metro size. U: CMA-year/property. V: land prices, structure cost. D: NHPI land/structure, assessment rolls. L: Stewart (CJE 2022): land dominant in some cities, not others; land-value data largely lacking. Against: land/structure splits mostly imputed; land is a residual of other constraints. I: land vs structure response to rate and immigration shocks. Src: ECON-10, CONS-10.

**H12 — Land-supply boundaries [C]**
M: restricted greenfield supply raises land values. E: positive on land price; ambiguous on total supply. U: parcels near boundaries. V: boundary dates, land prices. D: Greenbelt/ALR GIS, assessment. L: Deaton & Vyn farmland studies (land values only). Against: large unbuilt approved inventory implies designation not binding near term. I: boundary RD. Src: PLAN-6, A9.

### C. Regulation, approvals, politics

**H13 — Approval delay [B]**
M: long uncertain timelines raise carrying cost and risk. E: negative on starts; magnitude uncertain. U: application; municipality-year. V: application/approval/permit dates, units, staffing. D: BILD/Altus, CHBA benchmarking, MLURS 2022, municipal application data. L: GTA timelines 10-34 months (BILD 2022); CMHC: approval-delay index most associated with unaffordability; CMHC 2026: 10% more regulation ↔ ~14% higher prices (identification unverified). Against: industry samples; delay endogenous to complexity; approved projects stall anyway (H17). I: application-level hazard models; Bill 109/185 events. Src: PLAN-1, DATA-4, CONS-9.

**H14 — Cross-city stringency [C]**
M: binding density/use limits capitalize into prices. E: positive on prices, negative on elasticity. U: municipality. V: MLURS index, permits, prices. D: MLURS 2022 (single wave, on request), Fraser indices. L: GTA and Greater Vancouver highest index scores (100, 98). Against: most regulated cities are highest-demand; indices measured after outcomes. I: weak without a second wave. Src: PLAN-2, DATA-3.

**H15 — Citywide as-of-right upzoning raises infill [D]**
M: legalizing multiplex/rowhouse lifts redevelopment feasibility. E: more infill permits; small/slow in aggregate. U: parcel/neighbourhood-quarter. V: permits by type/location, prior zoning, land value. D: Calgary, Edmonton, Toronto, Vancouver open permits. L: Auckland (≈22,000 consents attributed; 3-bed rents 26-33% below counterfactual) vs Zurich (+13% on treated parcels in 10 years), Chicago (prices up, no short-run construction), Minneapolis (rents fell without supply response). Canada: descriptive only — Calgary 478 permits enabled; Toronto 452 multiplex permits May 2023-Nov 2024; Calgary repealed in 2026. I: within-city parcel DiD with switch-on/switch-off; synthetic control. Src: PLAN-3, INTL-1, INTL-5, A5-A8.

**H16 — Entitlement capitalizes fast, quantity slow [D]**
M: upzoning raises land option value at once; construction waits on financing, servicing, charges. E: land-price jump inside BC TOA rings within ~12 months; small permit discontinuity in 24-36 months. U: parcel. V: distance to station, prior zoning, sales, applications. D: BC Assessment, municipal permits (access untested). L: Zhang (Edmonton 2026 conference paper); Freemark (Chicago). Against: Auckland; minimums may not bind near SkyTrain. I: difference-in-discontinuities at 200/400/800 m. Src: CAUS-1, PLAN-7, A1.

**H17 — Approved-but-unbuilt [B]**
M: when approvals exceed starts, finance, presales and charges bind. E: ~zero marginal effect of entitlement reform on starts in high-inventory markets. U: CMA-quarter; project. V: approved units, starts, presales, cancellations. D: Toronto pipeline, Urbanation, CMHC. L: Toronto approved >29,700 units/yr vs ~16,000 built (2017-21); CivicAction 2,220 stalled GTHA projects / ~1.2M units. Against: approvals are lumpy and part speculative; "approved" ≠ feasible. I: conversion rates conditioned on feasibility across rate regimes. Src: PLAN-8.

**H18 — Permission without feasibility [B]**
M: fees, site standards, parking, financing and lot economics bind, not zoning text. E: low take-up in high-fee/high-process municipalities. U: municipality-year. V: permits for newly legal forms, DC rates, approval times, lot prices. D: municipal permits, DC bylaws. L: California SB 9 very low first-year uptake; ADUs high where fees relaxed. I: implementation variation under common provincial law (BC Bill 44, Ontario 2022). Src: INTL-2.

**H19 — Planning capacity and process design [D]**
M: understaffing and sequential review create queues independent of zoning text. E: negative on throughput. U: municipality-year. V: planner FTEs, volumes, stage durations. D: Ontario FIR, budgets. L: dispersion across GTA municipalities under the same provincial rules (suggestive). I: staffing shocks with municipality FE. Src: PLAN-4.

**H20 — Appeals and discretionary rezoning [D]**
M: third-party appeals and hearings add time and option-value delay. E: negative; magnitude unknown. U: application. V: appeals, outcomes, time to decision. D: OLT (limited), council records. L: Calgary reports 460 hearings avoided by citywide rezoning. I: survival analysis around Bills 108/23/185. Src: PLAN-5.

**H21 — Parking, code and type rules [E]**
M: rules favour a few capital-intensive forms and exclude small builders. E: slower response to price signals. U: CMA-year; builder. V: starts by type, rules. D: CMHC starts by type, bylaws. L: 32% of Toronto multiplex permits had zero parking. I: parking-minimum removal DiD (Edmonton 2020 confounded by COVID). Src: PLAN-11.

**H22 — Office conversion [D]**
M: underused offices convert at lower land cost within floor-plate limits. E: positive, small. U: building. D: Calgary program reports. L: Calgary ≈2,600 homes from 21 projects, $198M city funds. I: take-up analysis. Src: PLAN-10, A11.

**H23 — Homeowner-dominated local politics [D]**
M: concentrated incumbent opposition and ward systems give veto power. E: fewer permits where owner share and ward representation are high. U: council vote; municipality. V: ownership share, votes, electoral system. D: council records, elections data. L: no Canadian estimate found. I: close-election RD. Src: PFIN-7.

**H24 — Mandate fragility [E]**
M: mandates without local fiscal benefit trigger reversal. E: higher reversal in mandate-only regimes. U: reform episode. L: Calgary repeal (2026), Alberta order on Edmonton (2026), Bill 185 rollbacks, Berlin cap struck down; NZ MDRS reversal `[u]`. Against: Japan's national code durable. I: comparative case study. Src: INTL-8, A7-A8.

**H25 — Conditional transfers (HAF) [D]**
M: tying funds to zoning/process commitments changes municipal behaviour. E: positive on permits where commitments bind; additionality unmeasured. U: municipality-month. V: agreement dates, applicants (funded and not), permits. D: CMHC HAF rounds data, building permits. L: PBO: participants' permits +31% vs −15% non-participants (association); gains concentrated where rezoning pre-dated HAF. Against: selection; permits are the paid metric. I: staggered DiD among applicants; RD only if scores released. Src: PLAN-12, PFIN-10, DATA-2, B7.

### D. Fees and public finance

**H26 — DCs and stacked levies [B]**
M: per-unit charges payable up front raise feasibility thresholds. E: negative on starts; DCs reported at 8-16% of Ontario condo price (CMHC), all government charges up to ~24% (Altus, industry-commissioned). U: municipality-year; project. V: DC schedules, permits, starts, prices. D: Ontario FIR Sch. 61/62 (2019+), bylaws, CMHC DC study, building permits. L: C.D. Howe: 10% DC rise ↔ +0.45% prices (association); CMHC: cuts could raise viable projects by up to 14% in some cities. Against: accounting shares are not incidence; industry sources. I: DC-change event study with border pairs. Src: PFIN-1, PFIN-5, CONS-5, PLAN-9, B1-B3.

**H27 — DC incidence [D]**
M: with elastic site supply DCs capitalize into lower land prices; cuts then raise land values rather than lower home prices. E: pass-through to buyers between 0 and 1. U: parcel/transaction. V: land sales, DC changes. D: Teranet, Altus land sales, MPAC (paid). L: no Canadian design-based estimate found; Yinger model ~¼ on landowners. I: land-price DiD around sharp DC changes. Src: PFIN-2.

**H28 — Temporary DC cuts retime starts [D]**
M: time-limited cuts and scheduled increases shift permit dates. E: bunching at rate-change dates; 24-month cumulative effect well below the spike. U: municipality-month; project. V: DC schedules, permit dates, unit type. D: bylaws, building permits, CMHC starts. L: none design-based in Canada. Against: DCs at 8-16% of price could have level effects. I: bunching plus border-pair event study (Vaughan, Mississauga, Peel, Toronto). Src: CAUS-2, B2-B3.

**H29 — Growth does not pay for itself [C]**
M: if marginal servicing cost exceeds new revenue, councils slow approvals. E: negative on approvals where net fiscal cost is high; sign uncertain. U: municipality-year. V: DC revenue vs growth capital cost, tax per unit, permits. D: Ontario FIR, BC Local Government Statistics. L: IMFG (Ontario DC design underfunds growth) vs IMFG Quebec (fiscal gain from growth); municipal Bill 23 loss estimates are self-interested. I: fiscal-gap panel; Bill 23 revenue shock by pre-reform DC dependence. Src: PFIN-3, DATA-5.

**H30 — Servicing capacity [D]**
M: trunk water/wastewater/transit capacity gates development regardless of zoning. E: negative on starts where capacity is absent. U: municipality/service area. V: servicing allocation, reserves, permits. D: no consistent national data. L: none retrieved; BILD reports unspent DC reserves (cuts against cash-constraint story). I: staggered infrastructure-grant DiD. Src: PFIN-4.

**H31 — Tax ratios [D]**
M: high non-residential ratios cross-subsidize homeowners, lowering their cost of resisting growth. E: ambiguous. U: municipality-year. D: Altus ratio surveys, FIR. L: national commercial:residential ratio 2.82 (Altus 2023); no link to housing supply found. I: ratio-range reforms. Src: PFIN-8.

**H32 — Non-market withdrawal [B-C]**
M: end of federal social-housing funding (1993) shrank the low-cost segment. E: pressure at bottom of market. U: province-year. V: non-market completions, core housing need. D: CMHC custom series (discontinuous). L: non-market >35,000/yr (~20% of completions) in 1980s vs <1% 2007-15 (secondary); PBO: stock roughly stable at 600-700k. I: cross-province synthetic control (QC, BC continued). Src: PFIN-6.

**H33 — Public land and cost-rent stock [C]**
M: cost-based supply lowers rents directly and by competition. E: lower market rents with larger cost-rent share. U: city-year. L: Vienna WIFO (commissioned, correlational); Helsinki prices still rose. I: weak. Src: INTL-6.

### E. Construction, finance, pipeline

**H34 — Presale-financed condo model [B]**
M: lenders need presales; higher rates kill investor demand, launches stop, completions fall with a 3-4 year lag. E: GTHA 2025 condo sales −60%, starts −63%, record 7,243 units cancelled (Urbanation). U: project; CMA-quarter. V: presales, rates, cancellations, starts by intended market. D: Urbanation (paid), CMHC starts. L: descriptive; BoC SAP 2026-52. Against: national starts +6% in 2025 as rental rose; may be reversion from a speculative peak. I: rate shock × project type; cancellation hazard. Src: CONS-1.

**H35 — Viability gap binds [B]**
M: land + hard + soft + finance + charges + return exceed supportable value. E: negative on starts; shift to subsidized rental. U: representative pro forma by CMA/typology. V: costs, rents/prices, cap rates, land. D: Altus cost guide, CMHC, DC schedules. L: developer survey 49% negative on rental feasibility (secondary). Against: partly policy-made (H26); capacity story (H36). I: accounting pro forma plus policy discontinuities. Src: CONS-4.

**H36 — Labour capacity binds [C]**
M: retirements outpace entrants; capacity caps starts. E: BuildForce residential shortfall 67,600 by 2034 (slide-deck source). U: trade × province × year. V: employment, vacancies, wages. D: LFS, JVWS, BuildForce. Against: record starts in Calgary/Edmonton 2025; cost inflation slowed to 3.0%; likely binds only at peaks. I: shift-share with demand shocks. Src: CONS-3.

**H37 — Productivity decline [C]**
M: output per worker in residential building fell ~2.1%/yr 2001-23. E: large real cost wedge if real. U: firm/industry-year. D: StatCan linked firm data. L: Watt, Gu & ab Iorwerth (StatCan 2026): ~37% cumulative decline (figure reported two ways). Against: deflator, code stringency and typology shift may create a measured decline. I: decomposition by firm size, structure type, region. Src: CONS-2.

**H38 — Fragmented industry [B]**
M: 66% of sector employment in firms <20 workers; little capital deepening. E: negative on productivity, but small — reallocation to larger firms added <5% to growth. U: firm. D: StatCan linked data. I: firm panel. Src: CONS-7.

**H39 — Industrialized construction [D]**
M: plants need steady order books; volatility, code fragmentation and lender unfamiliarity block adoption. E: positive on productivity if scaled; magnitude unknown. U: project/plant. L: C.D. Howe: "not a silver bullet". I: staggered procurement pilots. Src: CONS-8.

**H40 — Rental subsidies and additionality [D]**
M: 100% GST rebate and insured long-amortization financing make rental pencil where condos do not. E: rental starts up (records in several CMAs, 2025); net effect on total starts ambiguous. U: project; CMA-quarter by tenure. V: starts by intended market, program uptake. D: CMHC starts by tenure, program data. L: no causal evaluation found. I: tenure × province × time triple difference (provincial matching varies). Src: CONS-6, B5-B6.

**H41 — Pipeline conversion [D]**
M: attrition and delay between permit, start and completion reflect financing, cost and local conditions. E: conversion falls with rates and costs; varies by city and regime. U: CMA-quarter. V: permits, starts, under construction, completions, costs, rates. D: building permits, CMHC SCS (34-10-0134/0154), BCPI. L: not found. Against: definitional mismatch, permit lapse; mechanical accounting links. I: descriptive decomposition; rate-shock exposure heterogeneity. Src: DATA-1.

### F. Rental market

**H42 — Rent-control design [D]**
M: exemption for post-15 Nov 2018 units raises expected returns but exposes tenants; decontrol gaps cut mobility. E: higher sitting-tenant rent growth in exempt units; supply effect ambiguous. U: unit; province-year. V: rents by tenure length, first-occupancy date. D: none open at unit level. L: no causal Canadian study found; Berlin freeze cut listings (Hahn et al.). I: RD in first-occupancy date (data feasibility unproven). Src: ECON-7, INTL-3, C6.

**H43 — New supply lowers nearby rents [D in Canada]**
M: local supply shock plus migration chains. E: −5% to −7% nearby rents (Asquith, Mast & Reed, US). U: building/tract-year. V: completion dates, tract rents, address histories. D: CMHC zone/CT rents; address histories restricted. L: no Canadian study found. I: event study around completion. Src: INTL-4.

**H44 — Short-term-rental restrictions [C]**
